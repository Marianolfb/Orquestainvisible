A heavy-hitting cover art

Buenos Aires, 2026 — By Julio Coviello
© Cari Aimé

With the Orquesta Invisible, we had been thinking about what the album could be called. Our bandoneon player Mariano Burnengo suggested a couple of ideas, and the name "Huella" (Footprint) began to resonate. Atahualpa Yupanqui's poem "Mi huella" expanded the entire meaning for us.

How can the cover art for a tango album called "Huella" look like?

But we had a problem. Footprint, path, trail—these are all words that remind us of our beloved folklore. But our footprint is on the path of tango. How can the cover art for a tango album called "Huella" look like? What image can represent the city? That's when I called Dema, a tango composer from Mataderos, a neighbourhood artist with a great capacity for synthesis. And we asked him.

Two weeks later we met, and with great emotion and many gestures, he told us his idea. "The footprint has to be on the asphalt, in the concrete, the name of the orchestra written with a stick, the footprint of a dog or a bicycle or a sneaker. Those are the footprints made in the city."

...that the album has weight. That the cover is a reinforced concrete tile that can accompany us in every performance, that can interact.

We loved the idea of a cement cover. A concept by Daniel López came to mind: "that graphic design has weight in kilos, that ideas have weight." He is the Director of Historical Heritage at the National University of Lanús and, with his team, built much of the UNLa sculpture park. His proposal is to move away from the ethereal nature of the image and materialise it. Applied to our music, let the album have weight. Let the cover be a reinforced concrete tile that can accompany us in every performance, that can interact.

Some things are irreplaceable. The digital era did not replace concrete. Recorded music did not replace live music. The most valuable thing about good ideas is when they can be carried out. We had to make a cement slab with the orchestra's name and a footprint. One day in March, before classes began, we went to the University with Fernando Estrup and Rodrigo Almonacid (violist and bandoneonist of the Invisible).

The most valuable thing about good ideas is when they can be carried out.

Dani López received us in his workshop and guided us to make this cover/object a reality. He set up a mould, had us cut a metal mesh, pour the cement, smooth it out, and then write on it with a stick and step on it with a bicycle wheel. A meeting filled with work, talks, music, and shared stories where our cover art was born.
