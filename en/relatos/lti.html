About La Tierra Invisible

Buenos Aires, 2026 — By Mariano Burnengo
© Cari Aimé

La Tierra Invisible is a small club located on a street corner in the Parque Chacabuco neighbourhood, in the city of Buenos Aires. I discovered it back in 2022, thanks to Romina Pernigotte, a tango teacher who gave classes before the Cañón milonga. It was there that I learned it was a space created by Julio Coviello, whom I already knew for his work as a bandoneonist in the legendary Orquesta Fernández Fierro.

Over time I left the classes, but the club became a reference point for me to listen to live tango. I had the privilege of seeing masters like Tata Cedrón or Tape Rubín there, and I even had the luxury of playing on one occasion with "Desborde", a student orchestra. Curiously, that was our last concert; the orchestra disbanded shortly after, overcome by the difficulties inherent in managing a group of twelve people. However, that end gave way to a new beginning.

In early 2025, La Tierra Invisible saw the birth of the Orquesta Invisible, which I joined from day one. This became our rehearsal refuge and the stage for our ritual every Wednesday morning: the first to arrive move the tables, lift the shutters, prepare the mate, and set up the music stands. I am fascinated by the contrast of this space. Rehearsal days have that rawness of shared work, while concert days, once a month, everything looks tidy and clean, ready to receive the audience. It's the comfort of playing at home. This dynamic reminds me of the golden age of tango, when each orchestra had its own club, its style, and its personality. Today, this corner is our trench.

In an era where tango is not mainstream —times of influencers, streaming, and a political context where art is often belittled— I believe that from our humble place we put up a fight. We make music that is over a hundred years old and we struggle to keep it alive by returning to the fringes: playing acoustically, without amplification, for fifty people, meeting on a corner in Parque Chacabuco to share wine and empanadas.

I feel a kind of nostalgia for the present, knowing I am a participant in current tango history. In times of globalisation, social media, and artificial intelligence, I don't know the real dimension of our contribution, but I am certain that, at least, we will have left our Trace (Huella).
